The game never touches Paula's audio registers for music. It plays through the operating system's audio.device: for each voice it hands the OS a sample pointer, a period (pitch) and a volume, and lets the device do the DMA. The sound banks (PrcSnd … UltSnd) are pure data — hunk modules whose data blocks carry the chip-RAM memory flag so AmigaDOS loads them where Paula can reach them; the player lives in the main program.

Two clocks, again

The player is a separate task driven by two clocks: when audio.device finishes a voice's sample it replies the request, and the reply advances that voice to its next note; a 60 Hz timer.device tick meanwhile runs the pitch and volume envelopes on the notes that are sounding, submitting an update only when a value actually changed. Note triggers are event-driven, expression is timer-driven.

A Soundtracker arrangement, one waveform

A course theme is the classic order-table-plus-patterns design: per channel, a list of [repeat, pattern] entries — the repeats matter, sections replay 4, 8 or 24 times — over byte-stream patterns of note and length commands. Every note in a bank is voiced from a single shared waveform: a standard ProTracker period table gives the semitone, and the octave selects the length of the looped slice (length << (8 − octave)) — the one-sample-many-octaves trick. A per-note volume envelope of ramp segments gives each note its pluck. Fourteen tunes ship across the six courses — two per course, with extra reels on Aerial and Ultimate — and the versions in this viewer are rendered by a from-scratch Go reimplementation of exactly this player, reading the notes straight off the disk image.
