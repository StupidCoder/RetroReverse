The race renders at 640×480 with bilinear texturing throughout. Vertex programs multiply positions as row vectors against a composite world-view-projection matrix; the 32-bit depth-stencil surface packs 24 bits of depth above 8 bits of stencil. Two render-to-texture passes run before the visible frame every single frame: the paint's mirror world and the cars' shadows.

The live reflection cube

The car's paint does not sample a pre-made reflection. Each frame the game renders the surrounding world into small 128×128 render targets — stored swizzled, in Morton order, like textures — and those targets serve as faces of the environment cube the body materials sample. The body shader goes further than a plain lookup: it reads a 64×64 tangent-space normal map, computes the reflection vector per pixel with dot-product combiner stages, and only then samples the cube. At rest (menus, the Studio) the same material slot holds the static showroom cube shipped in each car's texture bank.

Shadows from stand-ins

Car shadows come from a 512×512 depth map rendered from the light's point of view — but the real car models never appear in it. Each car carries a closed grey hull, a few hundred triangles up to the beltline, drawn only into the shadow map as the car's silhouette. Receivers compare depths in the map's own raw 24-bit units, with the comparison running before the bilinear filter — percentage-closer filtering, soft-edged shadows from a hard-edged map. A small bias band keeps the hull from shadowing itself.

Overlays and flares

Brake and headlight flares are additively blended quads pushed toward the camera by a depth-bias step encoded in their material state, so they hover just off the lamp without bleeding through geometry. Window glass blends premultiplied (ONE / 1−SRC_ALPHA) with the tint in the material's diffuse alpha. The starting gantry's lights are drawn as point primitives.
