The game keeps time by asking the CPU itself: its QueryPerformanceCounter is a raw RDTSC — the Pentium III timestamp counter — divided by a frequency hardcoded to 733,466,453 Hz, the retail Xbox's exact clock. There is no calibration and no fallback; the game simply trusts the silicon it shipped on.

The catch-up loop

Simulation advances in fixed 1/60-second steps. The menus clamp how many steps a slow frame may trigger, but race mode does not: however far real time has run ahead, the simulation loops until it has caught up, paying off the whole debt in full before the next frame draws. On real hardware that debt never grows beyond a step or two; the code, however, is written to chase any gap, no matter how large.

The audio watchdog

Once per frame, the sound driver checks that the audio effects DSP is alive by reading a ready marker (0x00CCCCCC) the effects program leaves in its scratch memory. If the marker is missing, the driver assumes the DSP died and re-runs the entire effects bring-up — including a deliberate 10-millisecond stall of the processor — every frame until the marker reappears.
