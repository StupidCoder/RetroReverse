The marble and the creatures ride the Amiga's hardware sprites — but the CPU never writes a sprite register. The copper does: sprite pointers and colours live inside the display list as data, rebuilt every frame, with the eight hardware channels re-assigned in bands down the screen so a handful of channels covers the whole cast. The stored sprite cells are laid out row-interleaved, which is exactly the hardware's sprite-DMA format — a cell is memcpy'd straight into a channel buffer at draw time.

Occlusion by subtraction

The display is configured with every sprite in front of the playfield, always — yet the marble visibly rolls behind the drawbridge. The engine does the most direct thing possible: it punches the scenery out of the marble. At load, each scenery cell's bitplanes are OR-ed into a one-plane silhouette mask; each frame, if the marble is behind a piece, the mask is shifted to sub-pixel position, inverted, and AND-ed into the sprite's own data words. Where scenery covers marble, the marble's pixels are simply deleted and the playfield shows through — per-pixel occlusion without any hardware priority at all. Sprite-vs-sprite layering then falls out of a depth-sorted display list: every drawable carries an isometric 3-D bounding box, bubble-sorted with a separating-axis comparator, and claims its sprite channel in that order.

Two clocks

The game runs as two cooperating tasks. A vblank-synced “Framer” task owns the display — it rebuilds the copper list and even keeps a histogram of how many vblanks each frame took. The world update runs in the game task and completes every two vblanks (~25 updates per second), so every script hold, actor step and palette-glitter phase is counted in world frames, at half the display rate.

Palette as level design

Colours 10–15 are not fixed: a per-course band table recolours them per vertical scroll position via copper waits, fading the accent palette as the marble descends — Beginner's emerald ice pit is a band, not a tile set. Rolling into an acid or ice pool overwrites the band with a pure red or blue ramp, and Ultimate's finale runs a three-phase gold rotation over its checkered goal — a classic copper sparkle, and the game's only ambient palette animation.
