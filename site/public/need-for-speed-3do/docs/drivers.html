Everything that moves on the road — the player, the rival, the traffic, the cop — lives in one pool of seven car structs, each carrying a driver function pointer as its brain. Three brains exist: the player's control path, one shared road AI, and a dormant-pool driver that counts down spawn gates and then rewrites its own car's driver pointer to the road AI. What makes a cop a cop and traffic traffic is not code but a per-car behaviour bitmask selecting which AI modules run: the pace governor, the cop module, lane steering, an oncoming-direction bit.

Two motion models on one road

Only the player is simulated. The player's car integrates forces into body-frame velocities through a full rigid-body model — orientation matrix, slip angle (the tyre smoke reads it), grip state, a 100-sample power curve and per-gear thresholds from the plain-text tunables. Every AI car — rival, cops, traffic alike — is kinematic: rails with a spring. Its position advances along a velocity vector, then a corrective spring pulls it toward a road anchor point; no forces, no slip, no orientation dynamics. The difficulty system rides the same split: per-skill Handling and Speed factors in the text files scale how much curves slow an AI car and what fraction of the reference top-speed line it may hold — the generic traffic class pins handling at 255, never slowed by a curve.

Eight lanes, scored

The steering module quantises the 40-metre road into eight five-metre lanes and scores each car's current lane against its neighbours: penalties keep same-way cars right of centre and oncoming cars left, the segment's own bytes narrow the drivable range, and an eight-slot occupancy map built from the other six cars feeds avoidance. The winning lane's centreline becomes the steering target. Oncoming traffic is the same code with one bit set — and its road-frame motion block negated wholesale.

A traffic system of seven cars

Traffic exists only in a window from 120 m behind the player to 324 m ahead; a car drifting outside re-enters the dormant pool and respawns ahead. Placement rolls an inline linear-congruential generator for a 50/50 direction coin (overridden in one-way zones), caps each direction at three active cars, and rolls the same stream again for the model and its livery — the four shared palette schemes each class carries. A handful of structs, recycled forever, reads as a living highway.

The cop's day

The cop idles pre-armed until the player comes within two segments at speed; that sets a pursuit flag on the player's mask. The bust compares a warning counter against a threshold from the constants files: under it a written warning, over it a speeding ticket — two different overlay cel families — while a pull-over choreography parks both cars at the roadside before the chase flags clear and the race resumes.

Crashes without scripts

Barrier hits are handled inside the continuous model, not by a canned state: at an instrumented 116 km/h berm impact the lateral position pins at the wall, speed scrubs from 32 to 9 m/s over a second and a half, the body yaws thirty degrees and the slip angle spikes — tyre smoke and all — with not one flag changing in the car struct. The rollover cinematics on hard frontal impacts are a separate, rarer path.
