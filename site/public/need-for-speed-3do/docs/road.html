A course is two files: a .trk with the road's spine and everything that rides on it, and a DriveArt packet with every texture, scenery model and sky. City stage 1 is a 9.7 km point-to-point road described by 2,400 segment records six metres apart — position, heading, per-segment bytes for shoulders and one-way zones — with the same slice construction shared by all nine courses.

A road that ships its own vertices

There is no extrusion math at runtime. Each group of four segments stores four road cross-sections as eleven world-space points each — centre, shoulder, curb, wall pairs, right side then left — already positioned in absolute coordinates. The geometry streams from disc in pre-baked sliding windows: each 0x3000-aligned TRKD chunk concatenates a group with its twelve neighbours, so one disc read hands the renderer its whole ±13-group surroundings with no stitching — and consecutive chunks repeat twelve thirteenths of their content. Disc space traded for seek time, on a drive that reads 300 KB/s at best.

Topology in the executable

Which points connect into which quads is not in the track file at all: the slice topology is three static tables in LaunchMe, shared by every course. A selector table picks a slice type by distance from the camera — a draw-LOD ramp where distant slices drop their wall points — and the face table's entries legitimately reach into the next group's first row, so wall quads span segment boundaries. Face materials remap through a per-group header into the packet's texture groups, each up to 36 cels deep: the animated billboards and flashing signs are texture groups cycling their frames.

Pace notes in the track file

Tucked after the segment array sits a table the AI drives by: 600 three-byte pace notes, one per four segments — a speed limit, an advisory speed that dips to walking pace at the hairpin, and a parameter byte. The opponent's throttle governor blends these notes with the live curvature it reads off consecutive segment headings; traffic simply cruises at the advisory speed for its side of the road.

The horizon is two rings of six cels

The sky is a panorama cylinder built from two rings of six 64×64 cels, stored rotated 90° so the cel engine's column walk becomes a screen row walk. The far ring is opaque — sky gradient and distant terrain — and the near ring draws over it: the treeline silhouette with transparent sky. Each cel spans exactly 15° of bearing, so the six-cel ring covers a quarter turn and repeats four times per revolution — the scenery loops every 90° of heading and is never noticed. The near ring's seventh cel is a pre-composed preview of both layers stacked, and Coastal ships a taller near ring for its ocean while collapsing the far ring to a single 4×4 destination-shading cel — a cel with no picture at all that darkens whatever is already in the framebuffer.

Roadside objects

Each course places around a thousand roadside objects from a table of 64 definitions: most are upright billboard cels with four mip levels, a few are full 3D models — (model, shape) pairs in the packet, the same format as the cars. Their placements are segment-relative, so the one definition table serves signs, lamp posts, bridges and the Thru-Traffic gantries alike.
